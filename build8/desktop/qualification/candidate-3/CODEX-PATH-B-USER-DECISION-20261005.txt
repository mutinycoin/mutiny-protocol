Architect decision: adopt Path B and amend hard stop E.

Evidence status: DESIGN authorization. This settles the execution-location policy dependency; it does not establish implementation or physical qualification.

1. Replace the execution-location requirement

Production admission requires authenticated qualifying physical TPM evidence, fresh proof of the required key relationship, and enforcement of one active voting lineage per qualifying physical TPM.

Software TPMs and vTPMs alone do not qualify. Remote node execution and TPM request forwarding are accepted limitations. They must not create additional voting lineages or duplicate reputation.

Do not reject an otherwise qualifying identity solely because its node software executes remotely or inside a VM. Do not claim that admission proves the entire node executes on the TPM’s physical host.

This supersedes the earlier requirement to stop solely because physical-host execution association cannot be demonstrated. It does not waive hardware authenticity, lineage uniqueness, accepted platform-profile checks or other admission requirements.

2. Enforce uniqueness in canonical state

Deduplicate the canonical hardware lineage across active membership, pending admission, supported EK identities, AK changes, key rotation and recovery.

Reject a second independent admission for an already represented lineage. An authorized recovery or rotation updates the existing lineage through its existing rules; presenting a duplicate identity is not itself recovery authorization.

Certificate reissuance, alternate encodings, supported RSA/ECC identities and additional attestation keys must not create additional reputation lineages. Use authenticated relationships and qualified platform-specific rules. Do not trust an applicant’s assertion that two keys represent either the same or different hardware.

If a hardware class cannot demonstrate the necessary uniqueness property, report that class as unqualified. Do not substitute “one EK hash” for “one physical TPM” without supporting evidence.

3. Complete fresh credential activation

Implement and verify TPM2_MakeCredential/TPM2_ActivateCredential admission workflows with validated EK credentials, exact AK public attributes and Name, fresh challenges and the approved network/Genesis/rule context.

Reject substituted keys, replayed responses, stale challenges and cross-context reuse. Ensure the evidence mechanism demonstrates actual activation under its stated verifier trust model; a claimant-created success transcript is insufficient.

Keep activation secrets, authorization values and private material out of logs, public witnesses and repositories. Retain the approved public evidence needed for deterministic verification.

Credential activation establishes the required TPM key relationship. It does not establish node execution location.

4. Preserve lineage through rotation and recovery

Use fresh activation when establishing a changed AK’s relationship to the registered hardware identity.

Bind the exact consensus public-key tuple through the typed enrollment or rotation statement, preserving required authorization and possession proofs. Keep Mutiny’s existing hybrid BOTH_REQUIRED signature policy.

Do not confuse the TPM attestation key with the consensus signing keys. A quote containing a public-key hash does not, by itself, prove that key resides inside the TPM.

A failed linkage must reject the attempted transition. It must not automatically grant a new identity, reset history, transfer bonds or require destruction of the existing hardware. Continue through the already authorized recovery rules where applicable.

TPM replacement or endorsement-identity changes require the specified admission/recovery treatment. Do not infer fresh hardware from a changed key alone.

5. Do not rely on hardware throughput

Registry and consensus rules must enforce the identity limit independently of TPM speed, network latency or process count.

Preserve existing objective continuity and signing rules. Add no relay-specific penalties, artificial timing tests, DRTM, TEE or PUF dependency.

PCR/profile evidence describes the attested TPM platform. Do not claim it also measures a remotely executing VM.

6. Required acceptance evidence

Demonstrate:

- Many concurrent clients using one qualified lineage cannot obtain multiple active or pending voting identities.
- Supported EK aliases, certificate reissuance and AK rotation cannot duplicate lineage.
- An unrelated software key cannot pass the required TPM-binding checks.
- Replays, substituted challenges and network/Genesis/context mismatches reject.
- Authorized rotation/recovery preserves the applicable bond, age, reputation and penalty history without duplication.
- Concurrent admission races, failed transactions and crash/reopen preserve uniqueness atomically.
- A forwarded, otherwise valid exchange is not rejected solely for forwarding and still yields at most one voting lineage.
- Existing A2 revocation, pending-join cancellation and monetary-rights protections remain intact.

Label simulations and TEST-VALUE results accurately. Physical qualification requires actual hardware evidence.

7. Continue the existing takeover assignment

Update the decision ledger, admission design, specification references, implementation and release-dependency list to reflect this amendment.

Remove “proof of node execution on the TPM’s physical host” as a launch dependency. Retain demonstrable hardware authenticity, lineage uniqueness, credential activation and physical qualification as completion requirements.

Continue authorized implementation, testing and review without another policy confirmation. This does not authorize TPM clearing, endorsement-seed replacement, production signing or Genesis sealing.
